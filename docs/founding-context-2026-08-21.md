# Founding Context — the motivation that was never written down (2026-08-21)

**What this is.** A dated record of a founding motivation for Yupi that
existed only in Tony's head until 2026-08-21, ten instances into the
project. It is *provenance*, not a governing document: it does not change
scope, claims, or any experimental commitment. When it conflicts with the
statutes (Part II, then Part I, then the proposal), the statutes win.

**Why it exists.** On 2026-08-21 Tony asked the tenth instance whether the
original motivation for building Yupi/Yupana had survived. A repo-wide
search found nothing: no mention of the paper, the pathogen, or the
proliferation argument anywhere in `docs/`, `CLAUDE.md`, or the memory
store — including the inaugural memory, whose stated charter was to carry
"reasons the docs don't" and which warns in its own text that "the reasons
die with the context window unless someone puts them here." The written
trace of this project begins at the *proximate* motivation ("an
interesting small model to train on my 4090", plus the impossibility-result
complement). Everything upstream of that was, in this instrument's own
vocabulary, **observation gap** — information the trace never carried,
unrecoverable by any reader on this interface, recoverable only by asking
the one person who held it. The founding memory predicted this failure
nine days before it was demonstrated.

Recorded now so that it can drift *visibly*. Silent drift is the failure
mode; drift is not.

## The paper — with a correction to the recollection

Tony filed the paper as "the Stanford macrophage (against e. coli) Nature
paper." Two details of that recollection are wrong; the argument it was
carrying is not.

**Citation.** S. H. King, C. L. Driscoll, D. B. Li, D. Guo, A. T.
Merchant, G. Brixi, M. E. Wilkinson, B. L. Hie, "Generative design of
bacteriophages with genome language models," *Science* **393**(6811),
eaec2657 (6 August 2026). DOI: 10.1126/science.aec2657. Corresponding
author B. L. Hie (Stanford); affiliations span five Stanford units
(Bioengineering, Chemical Engineering, Computer Science, Genetics,
Stanford Data Science), the Arc Institute, and the Broad. Local copies:
`docs/references/science.aec2657.{pdf,txt}`.

**Corrections.** (1) *Bacterio*phages, not *macro*phages — viruses that
infect bacteria, not immune cells; the paper contains no immunology.
(2) *Science*, not *Nature*. The E. coli target is real: *E. coli* C, and
two experimentally evolved ΦX174-resistant derivatives.

**What it reports.** Evo 1/Evo 2 genome language models — pretrained on
~2–2.7M phage genomes, fine-tuned on Microviridae, prompted with ΦX174 —
generated *complete* ~5.4-kb phage genomes. Thousands screened
computationally, ~300 chemically synthesized, 16 viable, materially
divergent from known natural phages (novel genes and regulatory elements,
variable lengths, one incorporating a packaging protein from an
evolutionarily distant phage, confirmed by cryo-EM). The headline
functional result: against ΦX174-resistant *E. coli* C, a cocktail of the
16 generated phages suppressed resistance in one to two passages and held
for at least five, while ΦX174 alone **and a cocktail of 16 natural
ΦX174-like phages both failed entirely**. Resistance-breaking variants
arose by recombination among the generated genomes; of 15 nonsynonymous
capsid/spike mutations absent from ΦX174, 14 had been generated by Evo.

**The sentence that carries Tony's argument**, from the paper's own
biosafety discussion:

> "the generative models themselves can possess inherent safeguards based
> on their training data; for example, we have previously shown that data
> exclusions successfully prevent the pretrained Evo 2 models from
> designing eukaryotic viruses, including pathogenic human viruses."

The state of the art in securing a generative design system is *choosing
what it was trained on*. That is Tony's observation stated as a defense:
the same lever — a narrowly defined, controlled dataset — is
simultaneously the safety mechanism and the threat model, and it holds
only for as long as one controls who does the training.

## The instrument's reading (this instance's interpretation, not Tony's words)

Fenced deliberately: what follows is the tenth instance's reconstruction
and may be wrong in ways Tony's telling above is not.

1. **The motivation is structural even though it is unwritten.** Yupana
   *is* the artifact class the proliferation argument is about: a small
   transformer trained from scratch on a narrowly defined, fully
   controlled corpus. It is the one instance of that class where the
   ground truth is owned and every question about what the model
   internalized has an exact answer. The three-gap decomposition is the
   recognition capability the argument implies — not "is this model
   contained" but *what did its trace carry, what did it organize, what
   does it expose*. The written motivations (epistemic observability, the
   constructive complement of the impossibility result) and the unwritten
   one demand the same instrument. That is likely why the wandering
   converged here after the original reason fell out of the trace.

2. **Why estar is the verb that gets outrun** (see the three-verb model,
   memory: `tonys-three-verb-model-of-safety`). *Ser* seguro lives in the
   endpoint (intrinsic properties of weights); *haber* seguro lives around
   it (perimeter, provision, gatekeeping). Both scale with capital and
   compute. *Estar* seguro is relational and situational, and
   relationships do not scale — they are artisanal, one at a time. So the
   gap Tony reports observing is not an accident of the current moment;
   it is what happens when the only two verbs that scale are the only two
   verbs being funded. The King et al. result is the biological
   demonstration: the perimeter is irrelevant when the artifact is 5.4 kb
   of synthesizable DNA, and the intrinsic property is a training-data
   choice made by whoever happens to be training.

3. **Yupi's place in the ayllu, on Tony's own framing.** If each project
   is one view of the interface boundary — PromptGuard defends it, Arbiter
   adjudicates across it, Mallku records reciprocity across it, epistemic
   honesty asks which claims can truthfully cross it, the impossibility
   result proves what cannot cross it, governance regulates the crossings,
   Indaleko is the boundary between a human and their own trace — then
   Yupi is the member that *prices* it: not whether information crosses,
   but the exact bits-and-mechanism cost of each crossing, in the one
   world small enough for the invoice to be itemized.
