# Literature check: methods lessons for exact synthetic worlds

2026-10-15. Uncommitted working note for skeleton v0.7, "Owed before drafting" items 1 and 2. Every arXiv id below was checked against its abstract page (`citation_title` meta) today. The non-arXiv item was checked through Crossref. I read abstracts only, plus search snippets for the critique literature. Nothing here is a full-text reading.

## 1. Does anyone already argue methods lessons for synthetic or toy-world studies?

Nearest neighbours, closest first:

- **Vafa, Chen, Rambachan, Kleinberg & Mullainathan, "Evaluating the World Model Implicit in a Generative Model"**, arXiv:2406.03689 (NeurIPS 2024). Exact DFA worlds (Othello, navigation, logic puzzles). Models pass the usual diagnostics (next-token validity, probes), but Myhill-Nerode-style metrics show their world models are incoherent. This is a methods critique *inside* exact worlds, but of a single kind: the standard measure is too weak. It does not cover objective-forced measures, overlap, seeds, collinearity, intervention confounds, or floors. **Partial pre-emption of the genre. Must cite.** The follow-up, **Vafa et al., "What Has a Foundation Model Found? Using Inductive Bias to Probe for World Models"**, arXiv:2507.06952, makes a similar measure-validity argument.
- **Zhong, Liu, Tegmark & Andreas, "The Clock and the Pizza"**, arXiv:2306.17844. In modular addition, small changes to initialization and hyperparameters yield qualitatively different algorithms. This is the closest prior statement that in a fully known toy task the *trained model* is the unit and seeds are not noise. It is about mechanisms, not paired statistical comparisons. Cite for §4.5.
- **Yuan & Søgaard, "Revisiting the Othello World Model Hypothesis"**, arXiv:2503.04421. A re-examination across models in the same synthetic world. Robustness of a finding, not methodology. Background only.
- **Joshi & Mueller, "Position: Causality is Key for Interpretability Claims to Generalise"**, arXiv:2602.16698. Recurring pitfalls in interpretability: findings that do not generalise, and causal claims that outrun the evidence. Its remedy is Pearl's hierarchy. It concerns LLM interpretability, not exact worlds, and it does not discuss overlap or seeds. Does not pre-empt. Useful for framing §4.6.
- **Hewitt & Liang, "Designing and Interpreting Probes with Control Tasks"**, arXiv:1909.03368, and **Belinkov, "Probing Classifiers: Promises, Shortcomings, and Advances"**, arXiv:2102.12452. These are the home references for "declare the baseline that makes a probe result uninformative" (§4.7, checklist item 5). They are written for natural-language probing, not exact worlds.
- **Raji et al., "AI and the Everything in the Whole Wide World Benchmark"**, arXiv:2111.15366. Construct validity of benchmarks. Its direction is the opposite of ours, because it questions whether narrow tasks speak to general claims. Background only.
- **Haklay et al., "Pitfalls in Evaluating Interpretability Agents"**, arXiv:2603.20101. Pitfalls in evaluating interpretability agents. A different object. Not relevant beyond the title.
- Philosophy of science: E. Sullivan, "Do Machine Learning Models Represent Their Targets?", *Philosophy of Science* (2023), on ML models as idealized toy models. **[Unverified: publisher page not checked; found only via a search snippet.]**

The audience's own justification can be quoted directly. **Agarwal, Dalal & Misra, "The Bayesian Geometry of Transformer Attention"**, arXiv:2512.22471 (the "Bayesian wind tunnels" paper; note its title), says verification "has been impossible: natural data lack analytic posteriors", and builds environments "where the true posterior is known in closed form". That is exactly the "exactness removes the excuse" premise that §1 needs. Other targets verified: Li et al. Othello-GPT 2210.13382; Shai et al. 2405.15943; Piotrowski et al. 2502.01954; Chawla et al. MetaOthello 2602.23164. Search snippets indicate that MetaOthello trained each model once with a fixed seed (42), "due to computational constraints". If that holds up on reading, it is a concrete instance of §4.5 in the target literature. **[Check the full text before citing it.]**

## 3. Reviewer-cited ids (skeleton item 2)

- **Johnson, Tarlow, Duvenaud & Maddison, "Experts Don't Cheat: Learning What You Don't Know By Predicting Pairs"**, arXiv:2402.08733, ICML 2024 (arXiv comment). VERIFIED. Single-response predictions do not separate aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty, so they train on *pairs* of responses. This complements §4.3 directly: the general fix is a second draw, and inside Yupana one step already suffices, because the mechanisms are sparse and the kernel is known. Cite it beside Bergna et al. It does not pre-empt.
- **Falck, Wang & Holmes, "Is In-Context Learning in Large Language Models Bayesian? A Martingale Perspective"**, arXiv:2406.00793, ICML 2024. VERIFIED. Martingale checks for Bayesian ICL, with a decomposed uncertainty. Does not pre-empt.
- **Chung, Song, Jung et al., "Quantifying Aleatoric Uncertainty of In-Context Learning for Robust Measure of LLM Prediction Confidence"**, arXiv:2606.19353, ACL 2026. VERIFIED. Uses "self-function vectors" to estimate aleatoric uncertainty in ICL. Does not pre-empt.
- **Parikh, Sai, Shivaswamy et al., "CATTO: Balancing Preferences and Confidence in Language Models"**, arXiv:2601.23096. VERIFIED. A calibration-aware token objective. It reports that preference alignment breaks the link between probability and correctness. Relevant to the post-training bullet of §4.1. Does not pre-empt.
- **Osband, Wen, Asghari et al., "Epistemic Neural Networks"**, arXiv:2107.08924. VERIFIED.
- **Osband, Wen, Asghari et al., "The Neural Testbed: Evaluating Joint Predictions"**, arXiv:2110.04629. VERIFIED. This is a synthetic testbed whose central argument is that marginal (one-step) predictions cannot assess epistemic uncertainty, so joint predictions are needed. That is the general-case statement behind §4.3. Our finding is that in a sparse exact world the marginal already suffices. Cite as the foil.

## 4. Verdict

The individual lessons are each well established in their home fields: seeds (Bouthillier, Henderson, Dodge), overlap (Petersen, D'Amour), objective-forced calibration (Guo, DPO), non-identifiability (Bergna, Johnson, Osband), and probe floors (Hewitt & Liang). Inside exact worlds, two lessons have prior instances. Vafa et al. show that standard diagnostics overstate world-model recovery. Zhong et al. show that seeds change the mechanism in a fully known task.

**I found no paper arguing the bundled claim**: that the standard methods failures persist *because* the testbed is exact, and that mechanism sparsity, the very property that makes exact posteriors tractable, both kills overlap for learner contrasts and makes general impossibility results (Bergna; Osband's joint-prediction argument; Johnson's pairs) fail to bind. The "cuts both ways" observation looks new. The search was abstract-level and limited to arXiv and the web, so this is a "not found", not proof of absence.

Narrowing advised: §1 and §7 should cite Vafa et al. 2406.03689 as the prior "exact worlds still mislead" paper and position ours as wider (seven causes, not one measure) and anchored in mechanism sparsity. §4.3 should pair Bergna with Osband 2110.04629 and Johnson 2402.08733, because these are the general results that the sparse world escapes.
