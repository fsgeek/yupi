> **Central claim.** Given a partially observed computational process, we can separate three questions that are usually conflated: what the observation interface makes knowable, what a trained sequence model makes representable, and what the model's exported prediction makes visible.

**Status.** This document supersedes the initial proposal, *Learning the Latent Structure of Formal and Computational Worlds*. The original question - whether next-token prediction can recover latent world state - has already received affirmative answers in chess, Othello, and hidden-state processes. This revision treats those results as the starting point rather than the proposed contribution.

# Abstract

Transformers trained only for next-token prediction can recover internal structure that is not explicitly present in their input tokens. Othello-GPT demonstrated decodable and causally active board-state representations, while recent work on hidden Markov processes showed that transformer residual streams can linearly represent the geometry of Bayesian belief states. These results make a simple demonstration that "a transformer learns hidden state" insufficient as a research contribution.

This project instead asks where information is lost along the path from a computational world to a model's externally visible output. It proposes a finite, operating-system-like stochastic simulator whose complete internal state and transition rules are known. The simulator emits event traces through deliberately varied observation interfaces. Because the hidden state space is finite and the generative process is controlled, the exact posterior over hidden states can be computed for every sampled history, as can finite-horizon predictive distributions over future observations.

Small decoder-only transformers will be pretrained from scratch on these traces. The experiments will independently vary observation interface, context horizon, entity naming, subsystem coupling, data volume, model capacity, and - in a later extension - hidden rule regime. Evaluation will compare the model's activations and outputs against exact information-theoretic ceilings. This permits a decomposition into: (1) an **observation gap**, consisting of information absent from the trace; (2) an **accessible representation gap**, consisting of inferable information not organized accessibly in the model; and (3) an **exposure or use gap**, consisting of information present internally but not causally used or visible in the immediate output distribution.

The intended result is not merely another synthetic world-model demonstration. It is an empirical instrument for measuring epistemic observability in learned models of computation. The first study is deliberately restricted to one structured computational world and models small enough to train on a single RTX 4090. Formal mathematics is retained as a subsequent replication domain after the method has been established.

# 1. Motivation and Reframing

Sequence models are commonly described as language models even when the sequence is generated by a game, a stochastic process, biological evolution, or a computer system. This raises a broad question: what predictive abstractions does next-token training induce when the source of regularity is not natural language?

The first proposal framed this as a question about whether a transformer could recover latent structure from observable traces. That question is important, but it is no longer open in its simplest form. Othello-GPT learned representations of board state from move sequences alone, and later work showed that those representations could be linearly decoded and causally implicated in next-move prediction [1, 2]. Shai et al. then provided a more general theoretical and empirical account: for data generated by hidden-state processes, transformer residual streams can reproduce the geometry of Bayesian belief updating, including distinctions that matter for the longer future but not for the immediate next-token distribution [3].

The opportunity therefore lies beyond asking whether hidden structure appears. A structured systems testbed can expose several boundaries that static board games and very small hidden Markov models do not isolate simultaneously:

1. **Partial observability.** A systems trace may not uniquely determine the machine state. The correct target is not necessarily the realized physical state, but the posterior over compatible states or an equivalent predictive representation.
2. **Observation-interface design.** Systems expose chosen telemetry. Fields can be added, omitted, masked, delayed, or coarsened while the underlying execution remains unchanged.
3. **Context as epistemic access.** A resource acquisition outside the context window can make current ownership unknowable. Context length and episode synchronization are therefore part of the observation model, not merely training hyperparameters.
4. **Compositional state and coupling.** Processes, locks, queues, devices, and schedulers can be independently structured or causally coupled. This creates a controllable setting for testing factorized versus entangled representations.
5. **Internal versus exported information.** A model may retain distinctions needed for later prediction without exposing them in its current logits. This creates a measurable analogue of the gap between internal epistemic state and text-only supervision.

The proposed project is the constructive complement of *Epistemic Observability in Language Models* [8]. That work establishes conditions under which text-only observation cannot distinguish grounded from fabricated outputs. The present project asks the complementary empirical question in a world where the hidden process is known exactly:

> How much latent structure does an observation channel surrender, how much of the available structure does a transformer recover, and how much of the recovered structure survives into the exported prediction?

# 2. Positioning Against Closest Prior Work

The novelty claim must be narrow and explicit. The project does **not** claim that transformers can learn world state, belief state, multiple rule systems, or factorized latent variables for the first time.

| Prior line of work | What it establishes | Remaining opening used here |
|---|---|---|
| Chess and Othello sequence models [1, 2] | Next-token models can track deterministic game state, and internal interventions can alter predictions. | Complete move histories largely determine board state; the observation gap is not the primary object of study. |
| Belief-state geometry in transformers [3, 9] | Residual streams can linearly represent exact mixed-state or belief-state geometry, including longer-horizon distinctions. | The studied generators are intentionally small. Observation interfaces, context truncation, structured variable binding, and system coupling are not jointly manipulated. |
| MetaOthello [4] | A single transformer can organize multiple worlds with shared syntax but differing rules or tokenizations. | Multiple rules alone are therefore not a sufficient contribution. The proposed work instead centers observability, structured partial state, and the observation-representation-exposure decomposition. |
| Factored representations [5] | Transformers exhibit a bias toward factorized subspaces, including cases where factorization sacrifices predictive fidelity. | A systems world provides explicit, controllable dependency graphs through locks, queues, devices, and shared resources, plus relational entity binding. |
| Computational mechanics and predictive-state representations [6, 7] | Predictive state can be defined canonically through distributions over future observations rather than inaccessible physical variables. | These theories supply the ground-truth targets and information ceilings against which learned representations will be measured. |
| Epistemic observability [8] | Output-only supervision cannot recover distinctions absent from the observed channel under stated assumptions. | The proposed simulator allows the observation gap, representation gap, and exported-interface gap to be measured separately rather than inferred indirectly. |

The closest conceptual competitor is Shai et al. [3]. The proposed contribution is therefore not "belief states appear in residual streams." It is the construction and use of a structured computational testbed in which:

- the observation interface is an independent experimental variable;
- the Bayes-optimal posterior and prediction are available as exact ceilings;
- context loss is modeled as information loss;
- variable binding is tested through episode-local entity permutation;
- subsystem independence and coupling are manipulated explicitly;
- internal representation is distinguished from causal use and immediate exposure;
- scaling is measured over model capacity, data volume, and predictive-state complexity.

This positioning is provisional. A systematic literature review should precede preregistration, particularly for recent work on predictive-state geometry, synthetic process interpretability, neural belief filters, and representation factorization.

# 3. Formal Problem Statement

## 3.1 Generative process

Let $X_t$ denote the simulator's complete internal state at time $t$. It may include thread program counters, runnable and blocked sets, CPU assignment, lock ownership, wait queues, outstanding I/O, device state, and scheduler metadata.

The world evolves according to a controlled stochastic transition process:

$$
P(X_{t+1} \mid X_t, \theta),
$$

where $\theta$ denotes a fixed rule regime such as scheduler policy. The initial study can hold $\theta$ constant; later experiments may make it latent or allow it to change during an episode.

An observation interface $I$ maps a transition into an emitted event record:

$$
O_t = g_I(X_{t-1}, X_t).
$$

The visible history at time $t$ is $h_t^{(L)}$, the suffix of observed events available within context horizon $L$. Full-episode observation is the special case in which the history begins at a known reset state.

## 3.2 Belief state and predictive state

The exact Bayesian belief state is:

$$
b_t(x) = P(X_t=x \mid h_t^{(L)}, I, \theta).
$$

Two distinct physical states may remain compatible with the same visible trace. Consequently, probe failure against the realized $X_t$ is not evidence of model failure unless $X_t$ is identifiable from the observation history.

For a target fact $Z_t=f(X_t)$, such as "thread A owns lock B," the relevant posterior is:

$$
P(Z_t \mid h_t^{(L)}).
$$

The minimal predictive object need not preserve every distinction in the raw simulator state. Histories are predictively equivalent when they induce the same distribution over all future observations:

$$
h \sim h' \quad \text{iff} \quad P(O_{>t} \mid h)=P(O_{>t} \mid h').
$$

This is the causal-state perspective of computational mechanics [6]. Operationally, the project will compare model activations both to exact hidden-state posteriors and to finite-horizon predictive vectors containing probabilities of selected future event sequences, following the predictive-state representation tradition [7]. This avoids assuming that implementation-level simulator state is itself the canonical representation.

## 3.3 Model state and output

Let $A_t^{(\ell)}$ denote the residual-stream activation at layer $\ell$ after observing $h_t^{(L)}$, and let $Y_t$ denote the model's immediate next-event distribution.

The information flow is:

$$
X_t \longrightarrow h_t^{(L)} \longrightarrow A_t^{(\ell)} \longrightarrow Y_t.
$$

The arrows after $h_t^{(L)}$ are deterministic for a fixed trained model. By the data-processing inequality, neither activations nor outputs can contain more information about a latent fact than the visible history contains. The empirical goal is to measure how close the learned system comes to the available ceiling and where additional loss occurs.

## 3.4 Three gaps

### Observation gap

The observation gap is the uncertainty that remains after seeing the available trace. For a target fact $Z_t$ it can be measured by:

$$
H(Z_t \mid h_t^{(L)})
$$

or by exact Bayes classification error. This gap is irreducible for any learner restricted to that interface and context.

### Accessible representation gap

The representation gap is the portion of trace-available information that is not recoverable by a constrained probe from $A_t^{(\ell)}$. "Accessible" is essential: a powerful nonlinear probe can relearn the task from incidental activation detail. Primary analyses will therefore use linear probes, affine alignment to exact belief geometry, and minimum-description-length-style controls, with small nonlinear probes treated as sensitivity analyses.

### Exposure or use gap

The exposure gap is the difference between information organized internally and information visible in $Y_t$. The causal-use question is stronger: does changing the relevant internal state change predictions in the direction implied by the counterfactual belief or predictive state? Activation patching between matched histories and carefully constrained steering will be used to distinguish decodability from causal use.

# 4. Research Questions

**RQ1 - Observability.** How do telemetry fields, context horizon, and synchronization markers change the exact posterior uncertainty over latent computational state?

**RQ2 - Predictive representation.** Do small transformers converge toward the exact belief state, a lower-dimensional predictive state, or another representation? Which distinctions become linearly accessible, and at which layers?

**RQ3 - Factorization and coupling.** When subsystems are predictively independent, do their representations occupy separable subspaces? When controlled dependencies are introduced through locks, queues, or shared devices, when and how does the representation cease to factor cleanly?

**RQ4 - Internal versus exported information.** Which distinctions are present in the residual stream but absent from the immediate next-event distribution? Which of those distinctions are causally used for longer-horizon prediction?

**RQ5 - Emergence and scale.** How do data volume, model capacity, context length, and predictive-state complexity determine the threshold at which the model approaches the exact information ceiling?

A secondary extension will study latent or changing rule regimes. Because MetaOthello already demonstrates multiple world models with shared syntax [4], rule variation will not be treated as the headline contribution unless the systems setting reveals behavior not captured by static game variants - for example, online regime changes, interacting policies, or uncertainty jointly over machine state and transition law.

# 5. Experimental Testbed: A Finite Computational World

## 5.1 Design objective

The simulator should be recognizably systems-like but small enough for exact inference. Real kernel traces are intentionally deferred. The initial world is an instrument, not a miniature production operating system.

A base configuration may contain:

- two to four threads, each executing a short finite-state workload;
- one or two CPUs with an explicit scheduler;
- runnable, running, lock-blocked, and I/O-blocked thread states;
- one or two locks with ownership and wait queues;
- one asynchronous device and a bounded request queue;
- stochastic completion events or scheduler choices;
- a known reset distribution and explicit episode boundaries.

Optional memory/page state should be added only after the base model is exact and tractable.

## 5.2 Hidden state

The complete state $X_t$ may include:

- per-thread program counter and execution status;
- CPU-to-thread assignment;
- scheduler queue and policy metadata;
- lock owner and wait-queue order;
- outstanding I/O requests and request lineage;
- device busy/idle state and completion phase;
- episode-local entity mapping.

Every transition will be logged internally, even when the observation interface masks portions of it.

## 5.3 Observable event records

A fixed record schema will be used across interface conditions so that the token alphabet and record length can remain constant. Fields unavailable under a given interface will contain a distinguished `MASKED` symbol rather than disappearing.

A representative record might contain:

```text
EVENT_TYPE   ACTOR   OBJECT   RELATED_ENTITY   LINEAGE   TIME_CLASS
BLOCK        T2      L1       T0               MASKED    NOW
```

A sparse interface may expose only `BLOCK T2`, while the remaining slots are masked. A causal interface may also expose the lock, current owner, and request lineage.

Using a fixed schema prevents changes in vocabulary size or sequence length from masquerading as gains due to observability.

## 5.4 Exact inference

The simulator will expose its transition matrix or equivalent transition function to an independent inference component. For every sampled visible history, the component will compute:

- the exact posterior $P(X_t \mid h_t^{(L)})$;
- exact posteriors for selected facts $Z_t$;
- the Bayes-optimal next-event distribution;
- finite-horizon distributions over selected future event sequences;
- where tractable, process measures such as entropy rate, predictive-state rank, and synchronization difficulty.

Tests will compare forward filtering against exhaustive enumeration on the smallest configurations. The inference implementation must be independently validated before any model experiment is interpreted.

# 6. Independent Experimental Manipulations

## 6.1 Observation interface

The primary independent variable is what the trace exports while the underlying execution distribution is held fixed.

Suggested interface levels are:

1. **Actor-only:** event type and initiating entity.
2. **Object-aware:** actor plus resource or device identity.
3. **Relational:** actor, resource, and the relevant related entity, such as current owner or waking thread.
4. **Provenance-aware:** relational fields plus operation lineage linking issue, completion, wake, and resume.

Two controls are important:

- **Entropy-matched irrelevant telemetry:** add a field with comparable entropy that is statistically independent of the latent fact of interest.
- **Redundant telemetry:** add a field derivable from already visible history.

These controls distinguish gains caused by raw token volume from gains caused by information.

## 6.2 Context and synchronization

Context is part of the epistemic interface. Experiments will compare:

- full episodes beginning from a known reset state;
- stationary sliding windows whose initial hidden state follows a known prior;
- windows with periodic state snapshots or synchronization markers;
- contexts that begin before or after causally important events such as lock acquisition or I/O issue.

For each condition, the exact posterior will be conditioned only on the history actually available to the model. This permits direct measurement of the information destroyed by truncation and the information restored by explicit snapshots.

## 6.3 Entity identity and variable binding

Entity identifiers will be freshly permuted across episodes. No semantic role will be permanently assigned to `T0`, `T1`, `L0`, or `D0`.

Evaluation will include:

- unseen permutations of familiar identifiers;
- identifiers withheld from training where vocabulary design permits;
- changes in the number of interchangeable entities;
- relational facts expressed independently of absolute names.

The target is not "decode the embedding for T2." It is relational abstraction such as "the blocked thread is waiting for the resource owned by the running thread."

## 6.4 Subsystem independence and coupling

A family of worlds will vary dependency structure while preserving much of the surface alphabet.

- **Independent condition:** two thread/resource subsystems evolve independently.
- **Shared-lock condition:** otherwise independent workloads contend on a lock.
- **Shared-device condition:** requests interact through one bounded queue.
- **Cross-resource condition:** progress in one subsystem depends on completion in another.
- **Priority interaction condition:** scheduler policy and resource ownership jointly determine future behavior.

This manipulation provides an explicit dependency graph against which factorized residual-stream geometry can be tested. It also positions the work directly against recent findings that transformers prefer factorized representations even when hidden dependencies make factorization lossy [5].

## 6.5 Model, data, and world scale

The first experimental matrix should remain staged rather than exhaustive.

Candidate model sizes are approximately:

- 1 million parameters;
- 5 million parameters;
- 20 million parameters;
- 50 million parameters.

Candidate training volumes are approximately 1 million, 10 million, and 50 million event tokens. Candidate context lengths are 32, 128, and 512 tokens, subject to pilot measurements of the world's synchronization horizon.

The initial screening can use one seed per cell to identify transitions. Confirmatory experiments should use multiple seeds only around the observed thresholds and for preregistered primary comparisons. This preserves feasibility on one RTX 4090 while avoiding a single-run result.

World complexity should not be summarized only by the number of implementation objects. Where possible, it will be characterized by quantities tied to prediction, including entropy rate, posterior uncertainty, predictive-state dimension or rank, synchronization time, and the degree of conditional dependence among subsystems.

# 7. Pretraining Pipeline

## 7.1 Model

The initial model will be a decoder-only transformer trained from random initialization. It should be deliberately small enough that every component can be owned and inspected:

- tokenizer and event serialization;
- corpus generation and data splits;
- sequence packing and context cropping;
- architecture and positional encoding;
- optimizer and mixed-precision policy;
- checkpointing and reproducibility metadata;
- activation capture, probing, and intervention hooks.

No pretrained language or code model will be used in Phase 1.

## 7.2 Data splits

Random trace splitting is insufficient because related episodes or repeated workload templates can leak structure. Splits will be defined across generative factors, including:

- random seeds and complete episodes;
- workload-program combinations;
- entity permutations;
- selected system configurations;
- held-out compositions of familiar components.

The test set should include both in-distribution histories and compositional generalization conditions.

## 7.3 Baselines

At minimum, results should be compared with:

- the exact Bayes predictor;
- fixed-order Markov or n-gram models;
- a compact recurrent baseline such as a GRU;
- untrained-transformer activation controls;
- shuffled-history and field-permutation controls.

A state-space model comparison may follow, but it is not required to establish the first result.

## 7.4 Reproducibility

Each run will record:

- simulator and corpus version hashes;
- complete configuration and random seeds;
- state-transition specification;
- interface definition;
- model architecture and training schedule;
- checkpoint metrics and hardware/software environment.

The simulator, inference engine, training pipeline, and analysis code should be released together if publication and safety review permit.

# 8. Evaluation

## 8.1 Predictive performance against the exact oracle

Ordinary cross-entropy will be supplemented by the KL divergence between the learned next-event distribution and the exact Bayes-optimal distribution. This separates irreducible event uncertainty from model error.

Longer-horizon evaluation will compare probabilities assigned to selected future event sequences or predictive tests. A model can match immediate next-event probabilities while losing distinctions needed for later prediction; those cases are central rather than pathological.

## 8.2 Belief-state geometry

For each layer, an affine map will be fit from activations to the exact posterior vector. Evaluation will use held-out histories and report:

- posterior mean-squared error and cross-entropy;
- geometric alignment after appropriate affine or orthogonal transformation;
- preservation of pairwise distances or neighborhood structure;
- layerwise emergence during training;
- sensitivity to probe capacity and regularization.

Where the raw state contains predictively irrelevant distinctions, activations will also be compared to finite-horizon predictive vectors and any tractable minimal predictive representation.

## 8.3 Fact recovery relative to the observation ceiling

For selected latent facts $Z$, the project will report not only probe accuracy but the fraction of trace-available information recovered.

Using cross-entropy as a proper scoring rule, a normalized measure can be defined as:

$$
\mathrm{OIR}(Z) = \frac{H(Z)-\mathrm{CE}(Z \mid A)}{H(Z)-H(Z \mid h)},
$$

where the denominator is the information about $Z$ made available by the trace and the numerator is the information made accessible by the activation under the selected probe family. The measure is undefined when the trace provides no information about $Z$ and may require careful finite-sample correction; it is therefore a proposed analysis metric rather than a settled primary endpoint.

Exact Bayes accuracy and posterior calibration will remain the most interpretable ceilings.

## 8.4 Factorization

Representation geometry will be tested against the known dependency structure. Candidate analyses include:

- subspace angles for independent latent factors;
- cross-covariance and canonical correlation across subsystem representations;
- additive reconstruction from factor-specific subspaces;
- loss incurred by forcing a factored representation;
- changes during training as dependencies are introduced.

The key comparison is not whether a convenient linear probe exists, but whether representation geometry changes in the way predicted by conditional independence and controlled coupling.

## 8.5 Causal use

Decodability does not imply use. Causal tests will prefer activation patching between matched, naturally occurring histories over unconstrained off-manifold edits.

A matched pair should differ in a target belief or predictive state while controlling as many nuisance variables as possible. Patching a candidate subspace from one history into the other should move the model's future predictions toward the exact counterfactual distribution. Interventions will be evaluated against:

- random-subspace patches;
- magnitude-matched noise;
- patches from histories with the same target fact;
- layer and token-position controls.

## 8.6 Exposure

For histories with identical or nearly identical immediate next-event distributions but different longer-horizon futures, the project will compare information recoverable from:

- individual residual-stream layers;
- concatenated layer activations;
- final-layer activations;
- immediate logits.

This directly tests whether predictive distinctions are retained internally but surrendered by the current output interface, as observed in simpler hidden processes [3].

## 8.7 Generalization

Generalization tests will include:

- new entity permutations;
- held-out workload compositions;
- longer episodes than seen during training;
- changed subsystem counts within architectural limits;
- interface changes that add or remove informative fields;
- optional transfer between nearby rule regimes.

# 9. Hypotheses

**H1 - Predictive-state alignment.** At adequate data and capacity, transformer activations will align more closely with exact belief or predictive state than with the complete implementation-level simulator state.

**H2 - Uncertainty preservation.** For histories compatible with multiple hidden states, a well-trained model will represent posterior uncertainty rather than prematurely collapsing to a single maximum-likelihood state. The activation geometry should track the exact posterior simplex or predictive-state geometry.

**H3 - Informative-interface response.** Adding causally informative telemetry will reduce the exact observation gap and induce corresponding changes in learned geometry and probe performance. Entropy-matched irrelevant telemetry will not produce the same gain.

**H4 - Factorization under independence, entanglement under coupling.** Predictively independent subsystems will be represented in approximately separable subspaces. Controlled coupling will require preservation of cross-subsystem dependencies; limited models may retain a lossy factorization bias before larger or better-trained models reorganize.

**H5 - Context as observability.** Removing causally relevant history will increase posterior uncertainty and impose an irreducible performance ceiling. Explicit synchronization or snapshot information will restore a predictable portion of that loss.

**H6 - Internal/external divergence.** Some distinctions required for future prediction will be recoverable from intermediate or distributed activations while remaining absent from immediate logits. Causal patching will show that at least a subset of these distinctions affects later predictions.

**H7 - Scaling toward, not through, the ceiling.** More data and capacity will reduce the accessible representation gap but cannot reduce uncertainty created by the observation interface. Scaling curves should approach interface-specific ceilings rather than a single universal performance level.

# 10. Expected Contributions

A successful project would contribute:

1. **A structured, exactly analyzable systems testbed** for learned predictive representation.
2. **A decomposition of epistemic loss** into observation, accessible representation, and exposure/use gaps.
3. **An experimental account of telemetry value,** measuring how specific fields and context markers alter both Bayes ceilings and learned representations.
4. **A systems-grounded study of factorization,** using explicit dependency graphs, shared resources, and relational variable binding.
5. **Scaling surfaces for small specialized models** over model capacity, data volume, context, and predictive-state complexity.
6. **A complete from-scratch pretraining and analysis pipeline** that fits on commodity research hardware.
7. **An empirical complement to epistemic-observability impossibility results,** showing precisely which distinctions the interface loses and which additional losses arise inside the learned system.

Negative results would remain informative. For example, failure to align with exact belief geometry under sufficient predictive performance would challenge the generality of existing belief-state accounts. Conversely, convergence to the exact posterior with persistent failure under causal interventions would separate representation from use more sharply than a success-only result.

# 11. Initial Scope and Milestones

The first paper should contain one computational world, not both systems and mathematics.

## Milestone 1 - Exact world and inference

- Specify the finite state machine and transition process.
- Implement the simulator and event interfaces.
- Validate exact filtering and Bayes prediction against exhaustive enumeration.
- Characterize observability and synchronization before training any transformer.

## Milestone 2 - From-scratch pretraining pipeline

- Implement corpus generation, tokenization, packing, training, and checkpointing.
- Reproduce simple next-event baselines.
- Confirm that small transformers approach the exact next-event predictor on the simplest interface.

## Milestone 3 - Observation and context experiments

- Compare sparse, object-aware, relational, and provenance-aware interfaces.
- Run irrelevant-telemetry and redundancy controls.
- Measure the effects of context truncation and synchronization markers.

## Milestone 4 - Binding, coupling, and scale

- Introduce episode-local identifier permutation.
- Compare independent and coupled subsystem families.
- Map selected transitions over model size and data volume.

## Milestone 5 - Representation, use, and exposure

- Align activations with exact posterior and predictive-state geometry.
- Conduct preregistered probe and causal-patching analyses.
- Compare residual-stream information with immediate logits.

## Milestone 6 - Replication and paper

- Repeat primary effects across multiple seeds.
- Freeze the analysis plan before confirmatory runs where practical.
- Release the simulator, inference engine, corpus generator, and training configuration with the paper.

A formal mathematical world becomes a subsequent study. Once the measurement method is established, a small proof or term-rewriting system can test whether the same observation-representation-exposure decomposition generalizes from causal computation to formal derivation. Keeping it out of Phase 1 removes proof-search and distance-to-goal confounds from the foundational experiment.

# 12. Feasibility on Existing Hardware

The intended models are small by modern standards, and event contexts are short. A 24 GB RTX 4090 should comfortably support the proposed 1M-50M parameter range, with mixed precision and ordinary activation-management techniques. The first constraint is more likely to be experimental breadth and exact-analysis complexity than raw GPU memory.

The available 64-core Threadripper and 256 GB of system memory are well suited to parallel corpus generation, exact filtering, and offline activation analysis. Before fixing the final matrix, pilot runs should measure tokens per second, activation-storage cost, and the synchronization horizon of the simulator. The proposal deliberately avoids depending on multi-node training.

# 13. Risks and Mitigations

**State-space explosion.** Even a toy systems world can grow rapidly. Begin with a strictly bounded state space and add one subsystem at a time. Preserve an exhaustive-enumeration configuration for validation.

**Belief-state complexity.** A finite hidden process can induce a large or continuous set of mixed states. Compute exact posteriors along sampled histories and use finite-horizon predictive tests when a complete minimal presentation is impractical.

**Probe overreach.** Powerful probes can learn the target rather than reveal accessible structure. Use exact ceilings, linear and MDL-constrained probes, held-out compositions, untrained controls, and causal interventions.

**Trivial local cues.** Facts may be decodable from the most recent event without a genuine state representation. Construct matched histories, remove or permute local cues, and test facts whose evidence is distributed across context.

**Off-manifold interventions.** Arbitrary activation steering can create uninterpretable states. Prefer patching between naturally occurring matched histories and include magnitude- and layer-matched controls.

**Identifier memorization.** Stable entity names can substitute for variable binding. Alpha-rename every episode and evaluate relational facts under unseen permutations.

**Novelty drift.** The interpretability literature is moving quickly. Re-run a focused literature review before preregistration and before claiming priority for any specific manipulation.

**Overextended first paper.** Rule changes, real kernel traces, formal mathematics, and architecture comparisons are all plausible extensions. They should remain extensions unless the base observation-gap experiment is complete and clear.

# 14. Working Contribution Statement

The proposal can be summarized in one sentence:

> We construct a finite computational world in which physical state, Bayesian belief state, and future-observation distributions are exactly available, then vary telemetry, context, coupling, and model scale to measure what information the trace makes knowable, what a transformer makes accessible internally, and what its exported prediction makes visible.

That is the claim the initial proposal was reaching toward but did not yet isolate.
