# Literature check: entropy vs. ignorance, and post-training, against exact ceilings

2026-10-15. Scope: the proposed study comparing per-class H(P\*), H(Q), and KL(P\*‖Q), with Q1 (does H(Q) − H(P\*) track KL, and does that depend on the interface?) and Q2 (what do SFT, sampled-token DPO, and a sharpening reward do to that relation, measured exactly?). Every arXiv id below was checked against the arXiv API on this date unless marked otherwise. Uncommitted working note.

## 0. One identity that frames everything

Log loss is CE(P\*, Q) = H(P\*) + KL(P\*‖Q). So "H(Q) − H(P\*) = KL(P\*‖Q)" is the same statement as H(Q) = CE(P\*, Q). That statement is the definition of **entropy calibration** (Braverman et al. 2019; Cao, Valiant & Liang 2025). In aggregate, Q1 is therefore an existing, named question. The per-class, exact-P\*, interface-indexed version is not. Near the optimum, with Q = P\* + δ, the entropy gap is first order (≈ −Σδ log P\*) while KL is second order (≈ ½Σδ²/P\*). Per class, the signed entropy gap is not determined by KL and can have either sign. The tracking question is therefore non-trivial, and it can go wrong in both directions.

## 1. The two named papers

**"Markovian Circuit Tracing for Transformer State Dynamics"**, Abdullah X (Project AWARE / Zephara AI), arXiv:2605.20824, May 2026. Verified, full HTML read. Note that the id is not titled as an "excess loss" paper. Excess loss over Bayes is one reported metric (mean 0.0138 nats across 6 HMM families × 3 seeds, ~275k-param transformers). The paper also covers ridge probes to exact belief vectors, recovery of the transition matrix from clustered activations, and state-forcing patches scored by KL to exact counterfactual targets e_i T E. It has **no** model-entropy vs. posterior-entropy comparison, **no** aleatoric/epistemic decomposition, **no** calibration, and **no** post-training. Pre-empts neither question. It is a citable "small transformers reach near-Bayes on HMMs with exact targets" datapoint, and a methodological cousin of our belief-geometry slice.

**"The Bayesian Geometry of Transformer Attention"**, Agarwal (also spelled Aggarwal), Dalal & Misra, arXiv:2512.22471, Dec 2025, v5 May 2026. Verified. I read the first ~100k of 118k characters. The headline metric is **mean absolute error between model predictive entropy and exact posterior entropy**, per position, in bits. They also report KL and TV to the exact predictive. The "wind tunnels" are bijection elimination, HMM filtering (forward algorithm), Gaussian regression, and associative recall. Example results: HMM K=20 MAE ≈ 7.5×10⁻⁵ bits; K=50 transformer 0.029 bits vs. capacity-matched MLP 0.40. **This is the closest prior art to Q1's measurement apparatus**: it computes both |H(Q) − H(P\*)| and KL against exact ceilings. It treats both as accuracy scores that go to zero together. It does not study their relation, the signed gap, or variation with an observation interface. It does not decompose aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty, and it does not post-train. It explicitly disclaims weight-space uncertainty. Paper II, arXiv:2512.22473 (verified), covers gradient dynamics. I could not locate Paper III, which is described secondhand as "production-scale LLMs"; it is **unverified**. Must cite. Pre-empts the measurement, not Q1's question.

## 2. Aleatoric vs. epistemic for LMs

- **Yadkori, Kuzborskij, György & Szepesvári, "To Believe or Not to Believe Your LLM"**, arXiv:2406.02543, 2024 (NeurIPS 2024 as "Iterative Prompting for Estimating Epistemic Uncertainty"). Verified. Uses iterative prompting to get an information-theoretic lower bound on epistemic uncertainty, treated as distance from the ground-truth response distribution. It works on real LLMs where the ground truth is unknown. It establishes that single-response entropy conflates the two kinds of uncertainty. Q1 adjacent; must cite. Not pre-empting, because there is no exact P\*.
- **Ahdritz, Qin, Vyas, Barak & Edelman, "Distinguishing the Knowable from the Unknowable with Language Models"**, arXiv:2402.03563, 2024. Verified. It uses a much larger model as a *proxy* for the true distribution, and linear probes on the small model's embeddings predict when the big model is confident. This is the closest conceptual precedent for Q1, and its explicit limitation is the lack of a known P\*, which is exactly what Yupana supplies. Must cite as the motivating gap.
- **Falck, Wang & Holmes, "Is In-Context Learning in LLMs Bayesian? A Martingale Perspective"**, arXiv:2406.00793, 2024. Verified. Uses synthetic exchangeable data with known generative processes and gives a decomposed (epistemic) uncertainty notion via the martingale property. Cite as an exact-ish synthetic precedent. It does not use the H(Q) vs. KL framing.
- **Müller et al., "Transformers Can Do Bayesian Inference" (PFNs)**, arXiv:2112.10510, 2021. Verified. Shows transformers trained on prior samples approximate the exact posterior predictive. Background for "log-loss training targets P\*".
- **Aswadi, Ma & Wei, "What does a Bayes-filtered transformer believe? A predictive Monte Carlo approach"**, arXiv:2607.17060, July 2026. Verified, abstract only. Argues that comparing a trained "Bayes-filtered transformer" to reference posteriors in prediction space is fragile, and recovers the implied prior and posterior instead. Relevant caution for any claim that reads Q's shortfall as a specific epistemic state. Neither question pre-empted.
- **Shai et al., "Transformers Represent Belief State Geometry…"**, arXiv:2405.15943, 2024. Verified. Residual-stream geometry versus exact mixed-state belief. I found no output-entropy analysis.

## 3. Post-training, calibration, entropy

- **Cao, Valiant & Liang, "On the Entropy Calibration of Language Models"**, arXiv:2511.11966, Nov 2025, v2 Jan 2026. Verified, partly read. Defines EntCE = |H(p̂) − ℒ(p\*‖p̂)|. Per §0, that is our Q1 quantity in aggregate, in disguise. v2 reports that **instruction tuning lowers entropy while raising log loss** (Qwen2.5 base vs. instruct, 1.5B–72B), leaving the largest models with too little entropy. The setting is natural text, with human samples standing in for P\*. The only known-distribution component is i.i.d. power-law theory. **Most important citation for both Q1 and Q2.** It is the direct natural-text analogue of what we would measure exactly.
- **Braverman, Chen, Kakade, Narasimhan & Zhang, "Calibration, Entropy Rates, and Memory in Language Models"**, arXiv:1906.05664, 2019. Verified. The origin of entropy-rate calibration: generation entropy drifts upward. Must cite.
- **OpenAI, GPT-4 Technical Report**, arXiv:2303.08774, 2023. Verified. Its MMLU calibration plot shows the post-trained (PPO) model is much less calibrated than the base model. Classic Q2 motivation, with real data and no exact P\*.
- **Kadavath et al., "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know"**, arXiv:2207.05221, 2022. Verified. Base models are well calibrated on multiple choice; RLHF policies are miscalibrated, which temperature partly repairs. Q2 motivation.
- **Zhu et al., "On the Calibration of Large Language Models and Alignment"**, Findings of EMNLP 2023 (aclanthology 2023.findings-emnlp.654). The URL appeared in search results; I did not fetch it, so the author list is **unverified**. Finds instruction tuning harms calibration while RLHF adds little further harm. Q2 motivation.
- **Kirk et al., "Understanding the Effects of RLHF on LLM Generalisation and Diversity"**, arXiv:2310.06452, 2023. Verified. RLHF reduces output diversity. Measures diversity, not calibration against a true P.
- **Zhang et al., "Verbalized Sampling: How to Mitigate Mode Collapse…"**, arXiv:2510.01171, 2025. Verified. Attributes mode collapse to typicality bias in preference data. This is relevant to our sampled-token DPO supervisor, which is a typicality-biased judge by construction.
- **Cui et al., "The Entropy Mechanism of RL for Reasoning LMs"**, arXiv:2505.22617, 2025. Verified. Entropy collapse in RL; entropy change is driven by the covariance of log-prob with advantage. Gives a mechanistic prediction for our sharpening reward. **Park et al., "Clip-Low Increases Entropy and Clip-High Decreases Entropy…"**, arXiv:2509.26114, 2025. Verified.
- **Omura, Fujita & Kataoka, "Entropy Controllable DPO"**, arXiv:2411.07595. Verified. Argues DPO's reverse-KL is mode-seeking. Predicts the direction of our DPO arm.
- **Baldelli et al., "Probabilistic Calibration Is a Trainable Capability in Language Models"**, arXiv:2605.11845, May 2026, v2 Oct 9 2026. Verified, abstract only. Fine-tunes LLMs on synthetic prompts whose target distributions are known, which is the *reverse* intervention: training toward calibration. It is the nearest "post-training against a known distribution" paper. It does not pretrain on the world, has no epistemic/aleatoric split, and does not use SFT, DPO, or RL in our sense. **Re-check before submission; it was revised six days ago.**

## 4. Simplex/Astera, 2025–2026

The arXiv author listing for Riechers and Shai, fetched today, shows the following:
- **Riechers, Bigelow, Alt & Shai, "Next-token pretraining implies in-context learning"**, arXiv:2505.18373, 2025. Verified. Information-theoretic predictions of context-dependent loss on synthetic sources. These are exact loss ceilings versus context, but the paper does not separate the entropy of Q from KL.
- **Piotrowski, Riechers, Filan & Shai**, arXiv:2502.01954, 2025, and **Shai et al., "Transformers learn factored representations"**, arXiv:2602.02385, 2026. Verified. Both are representation-side.
- **Balcells, Lee, Rastogi, Riechers, Shai & Poncini, "Large Language Models Develop Belief State Geometry In-Context"**, arXiv:2609.17376, Sept 2026. Verified, abstract only. **New since the founding review.** Six open LLMs are prompted with HMM data from 40 HMMs, belief states are linearly decodable (R² 0.83–0.99), and patching and steering preserve prediction quality. The abstract reports no entropy-vs-KL analysis and no post-training contrast. Six open LLMs probably include base/instruct pairs, so **read the full paper**: a base-vs-instruct comparison on exact HMM predictives would come closest to pre-empting Q2.
- **Shai et al.**, arXiv:2610.19238 (Oct 13 2026): an LLM-programming paper, not relevant.

I found no Simplex paper on output entropy versus the exact predictive, or on post-training.

## 5. Verdict

**Q1, already known or trivial?** Partly known, not trivial. In aggregate, "H(Q) − H(P\*) = KL" is entropy calibration (Braverman 2019; Cao et al. 2025), and the measurement apparatus of |H(Q) − H(P\*)| and KL against exact posteriors exists (Agarwal et al. 2512.22471). It is also known in principle that a single output entropy conflates aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty (Yadkori 2024; Ahdritz 2024). What I did not find: (a) a **per-class, signed** comparison of the entropy gap against KL, with the exact P\* known; (b) variation of that relation with the **observation interface**, holding the world fixed. The first-order vs. second-order asymmetry in §0 means tracking can fail in either sign even when total loss is near Bayes. Q1 should be framed as "exact, per-class entropy calibration as a function of interface", not as a discovery that telemetry and ignorance can diverge.

**Q2, done?** Not found. The phenomenon is documented on natural text without an exact P\*: post-training lowers entropy and raises log loss (Cao v2), damages calibration (GPT-4 TR, Kadavath, Zhu), collapses diversity (Kirk, Zhang), and collapses entropy under RL (Cui). Synthetic known-distribution work either trains *toward* calibration (Baldelli) or studies pretraining only (Agarwal, 2605.20824, Simplex). I found no exact decomposition of post-training's effect into the H(P\*), H(Q), and KL terms in a world with a known distribution, with arms for SFT, sampled-token preference optimisation, and a sharpening reward. The two items to check before claiming priority are the full text of 2609.17376 (base vs. instruct?) and the unlocated Paper III of the Agarwal/Misra trilogy.

**Positioning.** Present Q1 as entropy calibration (Cao et al.; Braverman et al.) made exact and per-class, with the observation interface as the manipulated variable. Credit Agarwal et al. for exact entropy-error measurement and Ahdritz et al. for the knowable/unknowable question that lacked a ground truth. Present Q2 as the controlled, exact counterpart of Cao v2, the GPT-4 TR, and Kadavath's post-training calibration observations. Use the mechanisms in Cui et al. and in Omura et al./Zhang et al. as *a priori* predictions for the sharpening and DPO arms. Link this to Mason & Anand's OLMo-3 telemetry observation as an analogy, consistent with the bridge-draft §0 discipline, not as a complement. Do not claim novelty for "entropy decouples from correctness after post-training". Claim novelty for measuring *how much*, *in which classes*, and *under which interfaces*, with every term exact.
